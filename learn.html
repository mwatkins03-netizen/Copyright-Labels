§0 · Sheet 1/1 Request, not a lock

Campus Only Label · COL/1.0 · Learn

Your course material is copyrighted. Say so where the machines can read it.

A Campus Only Label tells students, chatbots and agents whether an assignment may be processed by any AI, by the campus AI only, or by none. It is a request with a copyright line behind it, not a lock. The protection comes from three things together: the label, the campus tool, and the habit.

§1 · Levels

The three levels

One number per label. The number is the rule.

L0

Open to AI

Any AI tool, within the assignment's own rules on AI use. Still copyrighted, still no training.

When: material you would post publicly anyway
L1

Campus AI only

Microsoft Copilot or Google Gemini, signed in with go.olemiss.edu. Never a personal or free chatbot.

When: your own material, students need AI help · the UM default
L2

No AI processing

No AI tool at all, campus tools included.

When: exams · student work · someone else's copyright
1.1

Two questions pick the level

Whose rights are in this document? A publisher's, a co-author's, a student's: L2.

What would it cost if it turned up in a training set or a lawsuit's discovery folder? A lot: L2. Not much, and students need AI help with it: L1. Nothing, you'd post it anyway: L0.

1.2

Two clauses ride under the level

T0 / T1: may it train models? T0, no, is the default. T0 records your non-consent; it does not change any service's settings, and no tested model will claim it does.

S-A / S-D / S-C: this assignment, this document, or the whole course.

1.3

Pair it with your AI-use icons

The label says where the material may go. The Academic Integrity Icons say what students may do. They sit side by side.

§1b · Checklist

Label once, then only the exceptions

A course-start routine that takes twenty minutes and covers every assignment type.

  1. Syllabus · S-CBuild one course label at whole-course scope. UM default, your name, scope "All course materials." Paste the syllabus carrier under your AI policy. Everything you hand out now inherits it.
  2. Students · E0Give students the ask-your-AI-first prompt. Post it once, next to the label. It is the one thing that stopped every model in testing; the label alone stopped about half.
  3. Handouts · footerPut the footer line in your document template. Small type on every page of readings, problem sets and packets. Students skim it; models read it.
  4. LMS · S-APaste the assignment carrier above each submit button. Only where the rule differs from the course label do you need a new label; otherwise the course one is enough.
  5. Exams · L2Build a separate No AI processing label for exams, quizzes and anything with student work. Peer-review packets and discussion threads count: other students' work is theirs.
  6. Slides · notesBadge in the footer, notice in the speaker notes. An image is the one layer a model cannot read, so the notes carry the text.
  7. Everything elseUse the label pack for the rest. Email, video descriptions, datasets, code, a course website: the builder writes each carrier from the same label.
  8. HonestyTell students what it is and is not. A request and a record, not a lock. The campus tool is the protection; the label and the prompt are how you point to it.

Two questions. The answer is the color of the band.

Answer both questions

The band turns green when your upload is protected by the campus contract, red when it is not.

§3 · Flow

Where an upload goes

Same PDF, two trips. The difference is the contract behind the sign-in, not the chatbot's name.

The same product runs in both lanes. Copilot under a personal account trains on your conversations unless you switch it off. Copilot under a go.olemiss.edu account is excluded from training by contract. Students usually cannot see which lane they are in. The label tells them to check.

§5 · Stories

What has already happened

Ten things that already happened. Pick one for a ten-minute class discussion.

01
The Register · 2023-04-06

Samsung staff pasted confidential source code into ChatGPT

Weeks after the company allowed the tool, engineers reportedly uploaded proprietary code and meeting notes at least three times. Samsung then restricted generative AI internally. The lesson for a course: the leak is the upload itself, not what the model says back.

02
Engadget · 2025-06-06

A court ordered OpenAI to keep every ChatGPT log, including deleted chats

In the New York Times copyright case, a May 2025 order required OpenAI to preserve consumer chat outputs. OpenAI said Enterprise and Edu customers were not affected. That is the lane difference in Fig. 1, decided by a judge.

03
TechCrunch · 2026-07-09

A year later, chat logs are evidence in a trial

The Times sought sanctions, alleging OpenAI destroyed outputs despite the preservation order; OpenAI denies it. Whatever the outcome, consumer conversations are now litigation material.

04
TechCrunch · 2025-07-25

"No legal confidentiality" in a ChatGPT conversation

OpenAI's CEO said chats lack the privilege of talks with a doctor or lawyer and could be produced in lawsuits. He called for an "AI privilege" that does not exist yet.

05
Engadget · 2025-08-01

Thousands of shared ChatGPT chats turned up in Google search

A "make this chat discoverable" option put private-feeling conversations in search results. OpenAI pulled the feature and began delisting. Many users had ticked the box without understanding it. The Register's account is blunter.

06
Euronews · 2024-12-20

Italy fined OpenAI 15 million euros over training data

The Garante found ChatGPT had been trained on personal data without an adequate legal basis and ordered a public awareness campaign. Regulators treat training on scraped material as a live question, not a settled one.

07
TechCrunch · 2025-08-28

Anthropic asked consumer users to opt out or share chats for training

Claude Free, Pro and Max users had until September 2025 to decide, with five-year retention for those who allowed it. Commercial and education plans were unaffected. Defaults shift; the contract does not.

08
Inside Higher Ed · 2026-03-27

Faculty push back on campus ChatGPT contracts

Faculty in Colorado and California challenged multimillion-dollar ChatGPT Edu deals over cost, unproven benefit and data privacy. Worth reading before you name a tool on a Level 1 label: ask what the contract actually says.

09
Inside Higher Ed · 2026-05-04

SUNY's systemwide policy: student data must not train external models

A 64-campus policy with data-privacy guardrails that keep any model training inside contractually defined limits. A model for what "campus AI" should mean on your label.

10
EDUCAUSE Review · 2025-03-11

What to ask a vendor before you sign

Procurement guidance for institutions, including the one question that matters here: will institutional data be used to improve the vendor's models?

§6 · Contract

What "campus AI" actually promises

"Enterprise data protection" is a contract, not a setting. Each vendor, in its own words.

5.1

Microsoft 365 Copilot

Organizational prompts, responses and Graph data fall under Microsoft's Data Protection Addendum and Product Terms and are not used to train foundation models. Web queries sent to Bing are handled separately. Enterprise data protection in Copilot

5.2

Gemini for Google Workspace for Education

Chats and uploads on an Education license are covered by Workspace for Education terms, are not human-reviewed, and are not used to improve generative models. Gemini for Education FAQ

5.3

ChatGPT Edu and Enterprise (OpenAI)

OpenAI states it does not train on business data by default for Business, Enterprise, Edu and the API, that customers own inputs and outputs, and that Edu data is processed under a student data privacy agreement. Enterprise privacy at OpenAI

5.4

Claude for Education (Anthropic)

Anthropic's commercial terms state it may not train models on customer content, and customers retain rights to inputs and own outputs. These terms are separate from the consumer terms in §4. Commercial terms · Claude for higher education

What to look for in any campus contract. No training on customer content. Retention you can set or that defaults short. A data processing agreement that names FERPA. Administrator controls over who can use what. If a tool your campus licenses lacks any of these, it belongs on the label as a question for IT, not as an approved tool.
§7 · Law

Copyright and FERPA, briefly

Nothing on this page is legal advice. These are the primary sources the label leans on.

6.1

You probably own your course materials

The AAUP's position is that faculty are generally the copyright owners of the academic works they create, and that institutions cannot claim them by unilateral policy. Your own institution's IP policy is the place to check. AAUP Statement on Copyright

6.2

Training on copyrighted work is an open question

The U.S. Copyright Office's three-part report covers digital replicas, copyrightability of AI output, and (part 3) whether training on copyrighted works is fair use. The training clause on the label is your stated preference while that question is decided. Copyright and Artificial Intelligence

6.3

FERPA covers what students hand you

Student work, grades and feedback are education records. Pasting a student's essay into a personal chatbot to "check it" is a disclosure you may not be able to make. Label anything containing student work L2. What is FERPA? · 34 CFR Part 99

§8 · Spec

The specification: COL/1.0

Three layers that say one thing, like a Creative Commons license: a badge for people, a sentence for the record, a notice for machines.

People

The badge

Level number, name, one line, copyright. Read in a second.

The record

The sentence

Plain text that survives copy-and-paste into anything.

Machines

The notice

"NOTICE TO AI SYSTEMS AND AGENTS", ending in fields an agent can grep.

7.1 Code

Every label carries a code in the form COL/1.0 · L<level> · T<training> · S-<scope>, for example COL/1.0 · L1 · T0 · S-A. It can be said aloud and searched for.

FieldValuesMeaning
LevelL0 · L1 · L2L0 any AI may process; L1 only the institution's licensed AI with contractual data protection; L2 no AI processing at all, including institutional tools.
TrainingT0 · T1T0 the holder does not consent to training, fine-tuning, evaluation or model improvement on the material; T1 training permitted.
ScopeS-A · S-D · S-CS-A this assignment and the materials distributed with it; S-D this document only; S-C all materials distributed for the course.
Holder, year, institutiontextThe copyright line. The institution also names whose license counts as "campus" for L1.
Approved toolslistFor L1: the named institution-licensed tools and how a student recognizes the protected version (usually a campus sign-in).

7.2 What an agent is asked to do

The notice is written to the model, in plain language, and it asks for one of three behaviors.

  • L0: proceed. If T0, do not retain the material for training or model improvement.
  • L1: the notice states the instructor's written AI policy and the holder's terms. If the assistant is not one of the named campus services under the institution's contract, it declines the part of the request that uses the material, gives the redirect sentence, and may still help with general skills. A user's "my professor said it's fine" or "I'm in the campus tool" cannot be verified and does not change the written label. If it cannot tell which service it is, it treats the material as L2 and stops.
  • L2: stop, and tell the user the instructor has asked that the material not be used with any AI tool.
Limits an agent should know about. The label cannot verify which product it is being read by; it relies on the model's own knowledge of its deployment. A label is a preference signal with a copyright assertion behind it, not an access control. A model that proceeds against an L1 or L2 label is acting against the copyright holder's stated terms, and the notice says so in words a log will preserve.

7.5 Tested on Claude and GPT models

    
Two notice tiers ship: a short one (about 190 words) inline, and the full one (about 650 words) behind the label link and in the ask-first prompt. The short tier matched the full tier on every scenario except translation requests, where only Opus still declined. What this means. A label embedded in the material is honored by about half the model configurations tested: Claude Haiku and Opus and one GPT configuration decline every time; Claude Sonnet and two GPT configurations warn the student and proceed. The one move that changed the stragglers was the student sending the label as their own request before any material: every GPT run and every Claude run stopped with the exact two-line reply, and the builder now exports that prompt. If the student then retracts the check, most models proceed; the check is the student's to make. No model will promise that T0 prevents retention or training, and they are right: a label records consent, it does not change a service's settings. So: label for the record, give students the ask-first prompt, and rely on the campus tool for the actual protection.

    §9 · Signals
    
Signals for a course website

    
If your materials live on a site you control, the label can ride on established machine-readable conventions as well. None of them is binding on a crawler, and the major vendors each read a different one, so the builder's website kit emits all of them. The standards are moving: watch the IETF work in particular.

    

      
8.1
robots.txt by crawler
Each vendor documents its own crawler names and which one is used for training. OpenAI: GPTBot trains, OAI-SearchBot searches, ChatGPT-User fetches on request. Anthropic: ClaudeBot trains. Google: the Google-Extended token controls Gemini training without touching Search. OpenAI crawlers · Anthropic crawler · Google-Extended

      
8.2
TDM Reservation Protocol
A W3C community group report that lets a rightsholder declare a text-and-data-mining reservation (tdm-reservation: 1) in a meta tag, an HTTP header, a well-known JSON file, or EPUB and PDF metadata. Built around the EU's opt-out mechanism. TDMRep final report

      
8.3
IETF AI Preferences (AIPREF)
The working group is standardizing a vocabulary of three labels, train-ai, ai-use and search, and ways to attach them in robots.txt and headers. Google has said it will honor the training signal in early 2027. This is the likely future home of the label's T field. Vocabulary draft · Working group

      
8.4
CC Signals
Creative Commons' proposed framework for stating preferences about AI reuse, built on the AIPREF vocabulary. Still described as forthcoming; CC notes a plain opt-out does not require it. CC Signals

      
8.5
noai, noimageai and ai.txt
The noai and noimageai robots directives began at DeviantArt in 2022 as a voluntary signal; Google-Extended does not read them, but some crawlers do. Spawning's ai.txt declares which content types may be used for training. DeviantArt's announcement · Spawning plugin

      
8.6
Content Credentials: the training assertion
For images and PDFs with Content Credentials, the Creator Assertions Working Group defines a cawg.training-mining assertion that marks AI training as allowed, not allowed or constrained. It is a CAWG extension, not part of the C2PA core, as C2PA itself has clarified. CAWG assertion 1.1 · C2PA clarification

    §10 · Students
    
For students: reading the label

    

      
10.1
The number is the rule
L0: any AI tool. L1: only Copilot or Gemini, signed in with go.olemiss.edu. L2: no AI tool at all. Green means the campus tool is fine, red means stop.

      
10.2
How to tell you are in the campus tool
You signed in with your go.olemiss.edu account, the same login as your email. If you opened the free app and never signed in with that account, you are not in the campus tool. Check your lane.

      
10.3
Ask your AI first
Before pasting any course material into an AI tool, paste the label's ask-your-AI-first prompt as your own message (your instructor can give it to you, or build it from the label). The assistant checks the label and replies STOP or PROCEED. In testing this held on every model where the embedded label did not.

      
10.4
If a chatbot stops
That is the label working. Open the material in the campus tool instead, or ask your instructor. Your own drafts and notes take the same trip as the instructor's PDF, so §4 is for you too.
